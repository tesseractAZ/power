## 1.210.1

### The ledger's solar on the panel's own readings: logged as it is, and the mid-day shadow graded on it

- **What was wrong.** With the Cores dark, every ledger capture of a row planned on the panel basis logged "night-charge ledger: actual_pv_kwh WITHHELD — worst per-core PV coverage 0.00 < 0.9 over the scored span; …", while the row's PV actual is not the Cores' sum: since 1.204.0 it is the panel's own per-slot reading, admitted by the 7-day energy balance, and the rows hold it. And the winter mid-day shadow, which since 1.210.0 sizes on the panel-measured band, still measured its window's solar on the Cores' meter under the row's set-aside, so a sized shadow row had no measured PV to grade it against.
- **The log line.** On a row planned on the panel basis the Cores' per-Core coverage warning and their held-telemetry line are not logged. The panel path states its own outcome, once, in the order the row's note does: a warning "actual_pv_kwh WITHHELD for" the plan date, with the row's slot sources and the reason (per-slot coverage, held telemetry with the held devices, or a day the energy balance did not pass); an info line "pending the 7-day energy balance" while the balance has not judged the span's last day; nothing where the figure is written.
- **The shadow's window.** A cost leg sized on the panel-measured band of the row's own evening, on a row planned on the panel basis, has its window's solar measured by the row's own rule: the panel's per-slot reading over the window on the row's slot sources (a Core's own meter for a slot on its Core), at least 90 % covered, NULL on held telemetry, NULL until the energy balance has judged the window's day, and NULL where the balance did not pass that day. The band is read back from `shadow_arm_disposition`, from the cost leg's part only.
- **The window's way out of pending.** The row's usual capture, the evening after its span, comes before the energy balance can judge the window's day. A row captured with its own PV actual pending is re-captured once that judgement is read, and its window is measured again then. A row whose own actual was settled at that capture (withheld for a slot thin, unsourced or without its Core, or for the panel or a Core held, over the night) is never re-captured: the backfill sweep measures its window alone once a report that judges the day is read, inside the re-capture horizon, on the capture's own rule and inputs, and writes `shadow_window_pv_kwh` only, and only with a figure. It logs the figure at info and the reason a window stays NULL at debug, once per row per boot. A row first captured at or after 09:00 the morning after the window's day keeps what that capture measured.
- **What does not change.** A row on the Cores' basis: its coverage gate, its figures and both log lines, to the letter. Every column and value of the live overnight row. A cost leg on the Cores' band, a band of another evening, a row with no readable slot sources, and a panel-band leg on an evening the row itself was not on the panel basis are measured as before. No device write, push or announcement is added, and nothing arms a mid-day leg.
- **Detail.** Chapter 15 parts 2 and 3 of the reference.

## 1.210.0

### The winter mid-day shadow sizes on the panel's own solar readings while the Cores are dark

- **What was wrong.** Since 1.204.0 the overnight plan takes its solar from the panel's own per-slot readings when the Cores' band cannot be used; the mid-day shadow stayed on the Cores' band. With the Cores dark that band has no scored days, so every mid-day row read "not armed — the plan basis is incomplete (only 0 scored calibration day(s), need 4 …)" and sized nothing, while the overnight plan of the same evenings ran on the panel's readings.
- **The change.** Each leg the shadow sizes (the backup on options A and B, the mid-day cost leg, the shadow's morning refresh) makes the overnight plan's choice for itself, as of the evening that owns its window: the Cores' band where it passes and covers every connected slot, else the panel-measured band where every one of its checks holds, else the Cores' band. It reads no latch and not the live night's cancel, and takes the add-on's write mode whatever the leg's role. Only the shadow's own sizer asks for it.
- **Daylight.** The mid-day legs read the band through the day. A band hour is a fitted solar figure only where its clock hour has a fitted slope and a forecast GHI; elsewhere in daylight it is the typical-day fallback (0 W on a clock hour with no readings). A leg takes the panel band only when no hour from the plan instant to its horizon end falls on such an hour, or on one the band lacks.
- **The row.** A leg refused the panel's readings says why in `shadow_arm_disposition`, after the gate's own words ("…; the panel's own solar readings are not used: …"), and sizes nothing; a leg the panel band sized adds "(on the panel-measured planning band as of <evening>)", the band named rather than its slots, since a mixed band takes some slots from their Cores' own figures. A morning refresh planned on another band than the evening's records `stood` in `shadow_refresh_disposition` with the evening's figures, and its log line names both bands. A planning report not yet available or not listing its unfitted hours, or the Cores' band passing, leaves the row as before. No column is added.
- **What does not change.** The live overnight plan: its solar choice, latch, served plan, snapshot, ledger row and log lines. The live morning refresh stays on the Cores' band. No device write, push or announcement is added, and nothing arms a mid-day leg.
- **Known limits.** The shadow evening still runs once: a planning report missing at that minute leaves that evening's row as before. The measured window PV stays the Cores' own meter under the live row's set-aside, so while the Cores are dark a sized row has no measured PV to grade it. Neither band is calibrated per window, and the panel band's blind-band replay covers overnight nights only.
- **Detail.** Chapter 15 parts 1, 2 and 3 of the reference.

### Verification

7,160 server tests pass on CI; four more need pandoc or LibreOffice and run where those are installed. Two new test files hold the selection, each refusal, the daylight rule, the row, the unchanged cases and the live plan's lines. `mutate-panel-pv-basis.mjs` gains 41 mutants (group M), every mutant killed in a full run; 6,720 anchors resolve across 158 harnesses.

## 1.209.1

### The printable documentation's table of contents lists its chapters and sections

- **What was wrong.** The .docx and .pdf attached to every Release, and built on every pull request, showed "Table of Contents" over nothing. Pandoc writes the contents as an empty Word field, which only Microsoft Word fills (after asking). The PDF came from a plain LibreOffice conversion, which does not update a document's indexes, and the step that made it passed.
- **The .docx.** After pandoc, `scripts/build-docs-docx.py` caches one entry per level-1 and level-2 heading in the field (80 in this release: 7 and 73), each a link to its heading, level 2 indented, without page numbers; the field stays, so Word still rebuilds it with them. A heading without a bookmark to link, or entries that do not number the assembled Markdown's level-1 and level-2 headings, fail the build.
- **The .pdf.** `scripts/build-docs-pdf.py` renders it for the pull-request check and the Release alike: LibreOffice updates the contents, twice (the first update can move pages), then exports. Before LibreOffice starts, the script reads from the .docx how many entries the contents must list: those cached in its one level-1-and-2 field, at least one, one per Heading1 and Heading2 paragraph. It fails and leaves no PDF unless the updated contents lists that many, each with a page number, the pages rising; on a Release the PDF is then left out, on a pull request the check fails. LibreOffice works on a copy of the .docx in a throwaway directory that also holds its profile (started once before the conversion macro is installed: a first start rewrites the macro list), its temporary files and its lock file; the directory is removed and LibreOffice stopped when the script ends, also when the step's `timeout` stops it. A file LibreOffice cannot load, a LibreOffice still running after 200 s, and a stop each end the script with one line that says so.
- **What does not change.** The add-on: no code it runs changed. The documents' text, order, links and page breaks.

### Verification

The repository's build: the .docx caches 80 entries and the PDF's contents lists 80, each with a page number. ## 1.209.0

### The force-charge follows the backup pool, the ledger records the ON's fate, and a cost night's summary states its close

- **The pool.** While the pool alarm (1.208.0) stands for the panel, two rules that fail safe in opposite directions apply.
  - The charge rate counts a doubtful slot out: each connected slot the alarm names, whatever the verdict's age, and each it counts short without naming (the largest count in a dwell included); never under one Core. The live rate's bound, the plan's automatic charge cap and, where `FORCE_CHARGE_KW_PER_CORE` is set, the start's fallback rate take that count (unset: the fixed 10 kW).
  - The ON keeps a doubtful slot in: at the start, a re-issue or a line night's re-ON, a slot is left out only when the alarm names it, its newest counted reading is at most 15 minutes old, no return dwell is running and nothing contests the names (fewer or other slots in a dwell, or the newest reading showing every slot carrying its share).
  - With every connected slot left out the start is refused ("every connected battery slot is left out"). A line night pushes that after 10 minutes; the morning summary gives no cause.
  - The ON's readback check applies the same test. A slot it leaves out gets no re-issue, and its reading ON neither verifies the ON nor makes a failure partial; only another slot's does. A failure with no other slot reading ON lists the left-out slots too (one more log line and push sentence say which); with every slot of the ON left out, a failure is declared after the usual 15 minutes with no re-issue.
  - The log, while the start waits: "its charge rate counts 2 of the 3 connected Core(s), and an ON is not sent to slot(s) 1". The ON line adds "; slot(s) 1 left out", and while a Core is counted out a rate note that counts Cores says "Core(s) counted in the pool", not "connected Core(s)". The record keeps the slots (`forceChargeLeftOutSlots`, on the status route).
- **The ledger.** `night_charge_ledger` gains six columns: `force_charge_on_slots`, `force_charge_on_left_out`, `force_charge_on_retries`, `force_charge_on_verified_at_ms`, `force_charge_on_failed_at_ms`, `force_charge_on_failed_slots`. Retries is written 0 at the ON, so NULL means the fate was not recorded (no ON where the ON time is NULL too). Otherwise a verified time wins, also after a failure (a late apply); then a failed time; neither, the ON was never judged. Stamped at the ON, at each verdict and re-issue and at the leg's first OFF (a trailing leg's too), each time with the ON time and mode, restoring an ON stamp lost to a restart; never for a backup leg, at a re-issue the two-panel write block refuses, or, short of the first OFF, from a record that no longer holds the leg. A slot column holds slot numbers, ascending and comma-joined, or NULL; the failed slots include the slots left out at the check when no other slot read ON. The status route serves the columns unauthenticated.
- **The summary.** On a night with no outage line whose force-charge rode it (exceptions below), the morning summary states when the force-charge reached its stop, or when and why it was switched off early (after a verified ON, also a run to the window close), the ON's re-issues (after a failed ON, also "with no re-issue of the ON"), the slots the ON was not sent to, and the pool as read when the summary is sent beside the level the evening notice named: "the pool read 57% at the close, against the ~56.7% the evening notice named"; sent over 15 minutes after the close, "reads 61% now, past the window close"; with no fresh reading, "was not read at the close". A night whose force-charge failed its readback check, was never confirmed short of its stop, or never started as announced with the pool not read at or above its stop at the close, no longer goes under "completed" and the plain restore title. The title says the force-charge "did not run", "did not take effect", was "not applied on slot(s) 2, 3" or "not confirmed", or, failed on every slot and switched off at its stop, "force-charge ON not confirmed" (body: "was switched ON and its readback check failed"). An ON never confirmed that reached its stop keeps "completed" and the plain title; the body adds "with no readback showing every slot ON".
- **Why.** On 2026-11-16 a top-up that no slot applied closed at 49% against an announced 56.7% under the title "reserve restored" and the lead "completed". Its rate was timed for three Cores with two in the pool, and the ledger kept only that an ON was sent.
- **What does not change.** With no pool alarm standing, or none that can be read (said once at warn), every connected slot is counted and switched on as before. The OFF still goes to the slots the ON was sent to, so a slot left out gets none: the close does not switch off a Charge Now switched on by hand for it. The refusal on a Charge Now already ON (a left-out slot's too) and the capacity and standby figures are untouched. The summary changes no write. Unchanged summaries: a line night's, a reserve-only night's, that of a night a cancel or a grid loss ended, of one with no force-charge stop on record, of one whose force-charge never started where the evening notice expected none (its level was not the stop), and the mid-day one.
- **Known limits.**
  - A top-up needing under about 22 minutes of charge still has no room for a re-issue of its ON: the start has no floor for one.
  - The flat-pool hold of a line night's at-risk push, and the rate behind the clock a declined-start push quotes, take the count of the ON's targets, not the pool's.
  - A Core out of the pool that the ON's rule keeps in (counted and not named, or named on a verdict over 15 minutes old, contested or with a return dwell running) still gets the ON and its re-issue, and its reading ON still verifies the ON or makes a failure partial. A slot the alarm names wrongly, on a verdict the ON acts on, gets no ON that night.
  - A slot the check excuses as out of the pool is named so only in a verified ON's log line. As left out, the ledger and a no-line night's summary name only the slots left out at the ON; a line night's summary names none.
  - A slot left out at the ON is not switched on later that night when its Core returns; nor is one excused at the check, unless a line night's re-ON reaches it.
  - A loss the alarm has not yet raised is not counted out of the rate, and nothing says that the plan's charge cap counted a Core out: only the force-charge's log lines give a count.
  - The readiness gate, `forceChargeStateAt` and the mid-day hour mask read none of the new columns; to them an ON is still `force_charge_on_at_ms`, so a night whose ON never took is still served as a force-charge that was on. With the ON time restored from the record, so is a night on which the add-on restarted before any ON command went out.
  - The first OFF's ledger stamp is made once, after the OFF's device writes: a restart during them leaves a fate stamp already lost, lost.
  - A force-charge that ran and was cut short (the run backstop, a grid loss that did not end the night) keeps "completed" and the plain title; the body states the switch-off and the close.
- **Detail.** `docs/reference/15-2-night-charge-writes.md` and `15-3-night-charge-ledger-and-scoring.md`.

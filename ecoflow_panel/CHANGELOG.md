## 1.202.0

### The night-charge plan walks on the packs' measured capacity and drains the Cores' standby

**Night charge**

- Since 1.199.0 the outage-cushion line has been a percent of the packs' measured capacity (their BMS full-charge capacity, about 0.944 of nameplate on the reference plant), the basis of the reserve percent the panel holds. The walks that size the night still counted nameplate kWh per percent and drained the panel load ÷ the discharge efficiency with no standby; since 1.201.0 the cushion counts the Cores' standby for its hours, but the walks to the window close did not. On the reference plant a point of the pool held 0.870 kWh in the line and 0.922 kWh in the walks, and every hour before the close left out about 270 Wh of drain.
- **The fix.** The plan counts its pool once, after the refusals: the panel's nameplate figure × the measured capacity scale (`plannerPoolKwh`). Every walk, clamp and kWh per percent follows from it, and the line is the reserve plus the cushion on it, the same percent as 1.201.0's. The pool rides the scale on every basis the capacity tracker reports a plausible ratio on (`measured`, `held`, `min-known`). With no scale, a scale outside 0.5–1 or a scale of 1 the pool is the panel's figure.
- **The walks drain the Cores' standby.** The figure is 1.201.0's (the runway's for the connected Cores, read once by the planner; 0 W with none or with no reading). It is drained pack-side on every discharge leg: the islanded walks to the close, the share of each window hour the grid is not charging, the expected carry, the walk to a just-in-time start, and a line walk before its start and while latched. It is never drained from a charging gain (the grid feeds the Cores while it charges them), from the morning surplus, or in the cushion's hours, which start at the close where the walks end. Where a figure above 0 W is drained, a night the window cannot meet is sized at the window's full effort, as 1.201.0 does for the cushion's standby.
- **What keeps the panel's figure.** The live force-charge times its start on the panel's capacity figure, so the plan's mirror of it does too: the stated start is the minute that tick comes due along the plan's own walk. The cost room's conversion is 1.201.0's: the morning surplus is counted × pool ÷ panel in the cost ceiling, the cost target and the over-buy flag, and the de-bias bound is on the panel's figure, because the walks now drain the standby and the surplus does not subtract it. The headroom it converts can move, since the de-bias reads the walks twice: it applies only on a night whose walks hold its line (its gate), and it never takes the ceiling under the pool level the resilience buy lifts to on those walks (its bound). At 0.944 with 270 W in a write mode over 4–20 h, 88 of 9,571 cost ceilings rise and 3 fall, and 59 cost stops rise and 3 fall; 17 of the ceiling rises are one night (2026-11-06 13:00, at every hour) whose same percent rounds 0.1 point higher. On a scale of 1, 49 ceilings and 22 stops rise and none falls. Every fall is the gate (below, "The de-bias's gate"). The reference plant's two cost nights keep their ceilings, stops, writes and starts.
- **Worked example.** At a scale of 0.944 with 270 W, the live-shaped resilience night (a 16% floor, 4 h) keeps its 33.26% line and its 34% write. Its requirement rises from 24.30 to 26.42 kWh and its worst-case buy from 26.22 to 28.50 kWh. The expected pool at the window open (the median basis) reads 43.7% instead of 44.9%, and the worst-case low before the window 14.6% instead of 17.6%. The 2026-10-12 cost night keeps its 80.4% ceiling, 80% stop, 50% write and 02:43 start; the 2026-09-29 cost night its 77.7% ceiling and stop, 50% write and 02:54 start. The 2026-10-12 cost night's expected buy reads 34.10 kWh instead of 34.88: a point of the measured pool holds fewer kWh, which more than offsets the 1.2 points more the standby leaves to lift. With the pool at 74% at 21:30 the start is 9 minutes earlier (03:14 to 03:05, and 03:23 to 03:14 on 2026-09-29), since the walk to the start drains the standby.
- **A cost-carried line night's close.** A line night the cost force-charge carries (its stop at least 10 points above the line) stated its stop as the level reached. Its walk past the stop is now walked too: with no re-ON in cost mode, the house and a predicted EV draw the pool down to the close, with the reserve held at the written value. Where that close is under the line less 0.05 points, the plan names it, whatever the sizing, as a line-mode night's close is named: the rationale's NOTE, the 21:30 notice, the ARMED line ("Line NOT held: ~X% expected at the close.") and the card's outage-line row ("~X% expected at the close, under the line (the cost force-charge's stop is ~Y%)"). A night whose sizing held its line is disclosed (`closeUnder`, or `nearFull` on a line within 10 points of full); one whose sizing fell short keeps its caps note beside the named close. Disclosure only: the write, the mode, the stop and the buy are as without it. In a write mode over 4–20 h with 270 W, 23 such nights on nameplate closed under their line on 1.201.0 with nothing disclosed, all at 12 h or more and 22 with an EV session predicted, falling up to 31 points from the stop to as much as 11.6 under the line. 25 are disclosed on nameplate and 28 at 0.944. 70 and 57 more, sized short, name their close beside the caps note, at 6 h or more and up to 22.4 points under the line. None is at 4 h.
- **The shadow.** The mid-day shadow's held-import walk takes the plan's pool and standby; at the floors the held import less the free one is unchanged.
- **The year.** Against 1.201.0 at the same scale and figure, 4–20 h, 270 W, in a write mode, at 0.944: 188 written reserves rise and none falls; 127 holds charge and no charge holds; the line and every line force-charge ask are unchanged; no cost write falls; 59 cost stops rise and 3 fall (below, "The de-bias's gate"). 370 plans newly disclose a shortfall, and 135 that disclosed one no longer do. No charge night writes under its line's whole percent (up to 50%), and no line night closes under its line undisclosed, in a write mode, in advisory mode or on a reserve already reading 50%. On a scale of 1, with the standby walks alone, 188 writes rise, 76 holds charge, 22 stops rise and none falls. A worst-case buy, a requirement or an expected buy can be fewer kWh where the write is not, since a point of the measured pool holds fewer kWh. At 0.944, 1,116 resilience worst-case buys and 4,732 expected buys are lower (an expected buy by up to 7.58 kWh); on a scale of 1 no resilience worst-case buy or requirement falls.
- **Ledger.** `plan_pool_kwh` and `plan_walk_standby_w` record the pool the plan's walks counted and the standby they drained. Every sized row carries them (a charge or a hold): the panel's figure and 0 W where neither moved. A refused row and earlier rows read NULL. `pool_full_kwh` stays the panel's figure. `cushion_pool_scale` is now also the pool's scale, so a legacy-band or disabled row whose pool moved carries it too. `CURRENT_ALGO_VERSION` stays 3: every verdict keeps its rule on the row's own line.
- **The measured-basis contract.** The pool is counted on one line of the sizing, after the refusals. `index.ts` counts no pool of its own and hands the shadow the plan's; the force-charge tick's module never names it. The contract pins each.

**What is disclosed**

- **The live plant.** While its night plans are blocked by an incomplete calibration basis, nothing is written and none of this runs on its charge path. Once plans run, at 0.944 and 270 W, the live-shaped resilience nights write what they wrote on 1.201.0 (34%) and their worst-case buys rise: 26.22 to 28.50 kWh on 2026-10-12, 22.59 to 23.66 kWh on 2026-09-29. Across the year at 0.944, 188 writes rise and none falls. The reference plant's two cost nights (2026-10-12 and 2026-09-29) keep their ceilings, stops, writes and starts.
- **Claims where 1.201.0 disclosed.** At 0.944, 135 charge plans that disclosed a shortfall on 1.201.0 now claim the line: 91 the charge rate and 37 a predicted EV sharing the grid input held under it on 1.201.0's walks, and 7 line nights closed under it; their walks on the measured pool reach it. Each claim is as good as the scale. On a held or lowest-known ratio, or with a BMS that over-reads its capacity, it can be optimistic, and a charge night that closes under its line is a realized strike. On a held (0.92) or lowest-known (0.9) scale the count is 193 and 250.
- **The de-bias's gate.** At 0.944 with 270 W in a write mode over 4–20 h, 3 cost stops fall, by up to 3.4 points (2027-02-10 +1 01:30 at 8 h: 80% to 76.6%; 2027-02-19 21:30 at 10 h; 2026-11-30 +1 01:30 at 13 h), and no cost write. Each is a night 1.201.0's walks disclosed short of its line that the measured pool's walks hold, so it gets the de-bias that every night holding its line gets. The de-bias never takes a ceiling under the pool level the resilience buy lifts to (to the tenth of a point the ceiling is stated in), and each write it lowers below is its line's whole percent. On a held (0.92) or lowest-known (0.9) scale at 270 W the same gate lowers 4 and 3 stops, and 1 and 2 cost writes: 2026-10-13 +1 01:30 at 6 h from 50% to 43% and 44%, after which the cost force-charge no longer rides it, and 2027-07-12 +1 01:30 at 7 h from 45% to 43%. The falls move with the standby figure. At 0.944 the gate lowers 3 stops at 0 W and 2 at 336 W; one cost write at 90 W (2027-07-12 +1 01:30 at 8 h, 45% to 44%) and one at 336 W (2026-10-13 +1 01:30 at 6 h, 50% to 43%, after which the cost force-charge no longer rides it); and at 90 W two ceilings of nights the line force-charge carries: one hands its night to it (2027-05-07 21:30 at 12 h: the 73.8% cost stop gives way to a 68.2% ceiling), the other lowers the line force-charge's start target from 73.8% to 73% (2027-07-23 21:30 at 18 h), still 5 points above its line's whole percent. On a held or lowest-known scale at those figures the same gate lowers more, every one pinned in `measuredPlannerWalks.test.ts` T6b: a write to its line's whole percent, a stop or ceiling at or above the level the resilience buy lifts to (to the tenth of a point), a line night's start target at or above its line's whole percent + 5. None is at 4 h, and no resilience write falls at any of these figures or scales.
- **More drain can make a claim.** As the standby figure rises from 0 to 270 W at 0.944, 22 disclosed line nights in a write mode and 19 on a standing 50% are carried instead. Either 1.200.0's re-ON walk switches a latched night back on, or the higher line hands a cost-carried night to the line force-charge's own walk; each holds its line by its own walk. As the figure rises, a hold's setpoint (its no-buy prediction), the expected buy, its level and the arrival can fall. What is written and sized does not.
- **Minimum-buy holds.** At five scales from 1 to 0.8 and cushions of 4–20 h with 270 W in a write mode, 85 islanded holds have a requirement. Each is a minimum-buy hold, and its rationale says so. Its pack at the close is under the line by at most its need, which is at most `ARB_MIN_BUY_KWH` × the charge efficiency (at most 1.194 points of its pool).
- **The tick stays on nameplate.** The live force-charge times its start on the panel's nameplate figure. That figure counts more energy per point than the measured pool, so a tick on the measured pool would come due no earlier. The plan's stated start and close follow the tick that runs.
- **The expected buy** is an estimate of the energy into the Cores, never a meter reading. The grid meter also counts the house load the grid serves during the window, and the Cores' own standby while the grid charges them.
- **The 270 W is a pack-only fit.** On three later spans the implied overhead averaged 303 W (266–336). The walks now drain the figure for every hour to the close, so at 303 W each such hour drains 33 Wh more than the plan counts.
- **Nothing here has run on the plant.** Every figure here is the planner on the year of fixture nights.

## 1.201.0

### The outage cushion counts the Cores' own standby draw for its hours

**Night charge**

- The islanded-outage cushion was sized from the panel load alone: the configured hours × the 7-day mean panel load × `ARB_ISLANDED_LOAD_SAFETY` ÷ the discharge efficiency. On an island the Cores' inverters form the grid, and their own electronics draw whether or not the backup circuits load. That draw never appears in the panel load. The runway has counted it since 1.187.19 as its pool overhead (≈ 267 W plus the load ÷ 0.94, fitted on 30 pack-only discharge spans; 270 W at the defaults while the panel reports a pool), but the cushion did not. On the live-shaped night (a 16% floor, 4 h × 2.62 kW × 1.25 ÷ 0.94 = 13.94 kWh) the cushion covered 3.71 of its 4 h at the safety-factored load plus 270 W.
- **The fix.** An islanded-outage cushion now adds the Cores' standby for its hours: cushion = hours × (the safety-factored panel load ÷ the discharge efficiency + the standby), rounded to 0.01 kWh. The standby is the runway's figure for the Cores the panel counts as connected, passed to the planner as one number. It is not multiplied by `ARB_ISLANDED_LOAD_SAFETY`, which covers the panel load's CT scaling and its spread about a 7-day mean, and not divided by the discharge efficiency, because the figure is already on the pack's side. At the runway's default marginal drain efficiency this is the runway's own drain for the cushion's hours. The line converts the whole cushion on the measured capacity once, as in 1.199.0. At 270 W the line rises about 1.24 points at 4 h and 2.48 at 8 h on the reference pool at a scale of 0.944 (1.17 and 2.34 on nameplate).
- **Worked example.** The same night adds 4 h × 270 W = 1.08 kWh, a cushion of 15.02 kWh. At 0.944 the line moves from 32.02% to 33.26%, and a resilience write from 33% to 34%, which holds 18% × 86.999 = 15.66 kWh above the floor; the requirement goes from 23.38 to 24.30 kWh and the worst-case buy from 25.22 to 26.22 kWh. On nameplate, the basis until a pack capacity ratio has been measured, the line moves from 31.13% to 32.30% and the write from 32% to 33%. The live-shaped cost plans (a 77.7–90% target) still write 50%, buy what they did and expect what they did; where they need a lift, the requirement rises 0.86–0.92 kWh. The rationale names the share as the runway's figure, part of what the line is sized to carry: "(15 kWh, 1.1 kWh of it the runway's 270 W standby figure, 17.3% of the packs' measured capacity at ×0.944 of nameplate)". That is what the line is sized on, not how long an island lasts.
- **Where the figure comes from.** The planner evaluates the runway's overhead rule for the Cores the house panel's cushion tracker counted, from the same reading as the pool capacity and the measured scale, before its first `await`: the larger of `RUNWAY_POOL_OVERHEAD_FLOOR_W` (270 W) and `RUNWAY_CORE_OVERHEAD_W` (90 W) × the connected Cores. The count is the panel's own connection flag, so a cloud-dark Core still counts; a tracker error counts no Core, which is the floor. At the defaults the figure is 270 W for 0 to 3 connected Cores (the panel has three source slots). The log states the figure when it or the Core count changes: "night-charge: the Cores' standby figure for the outage cushion is 270 W (the runway's figure for 3 connected Core(s)); an islanded-outage cushion counts it for its hours". Whether a night counted it is the plan's own `cushionStandby` (`w`, `kwh`, `basis`), which `/api/night-charge/status` shows.
- **Fail-safe.** With no figure, the plan is 1.200.1's byte for byte, with no new key. A figure that is not a finite number from 0 to 1500 W counts as 0 W and is recorded `implausible`; 0 W moves no number and no word. Both overhead options at 0 remove the standby from the runway and the cushion alike. A legacy-pct band and a disabled cushion never take it.
- **Past the pool.** A figure that carries the line above 100% of the pool makes the night unmeetable. Such a night reported a legacy proxy (the pool's capacity less the pack it would hold with no buy), which sits below what the window lifts just short of 100%, so a higher figure could lower the requirement and the worst-case buy, and under a large `ARB_MIN_BUY_KWH` turn a charge night into a hold. Where a figure above 0 W is counted, an unmeetable night is now sized at the window's full effort: on 2027-02-09 21:30 at 12 h and a scale of 0.75, from 560 to 570 W the requirement goes from 84.67 to 99.04 kWh (61.27 on the proxy). With no figure or 0 W the proxy stays.
- **One cushion, every reader.** The hold test, the requirement, `cushionShortfall`, the written setpoint, the mid-day leg's T_mid line term, the backup leg's walk and the line force-charge's ask all read the one line. A pack that sat between the two lines at the window close now charges (2027-05-20 21:30 at 0.944: a hold becomes a 1.76 kWh charge). The mid-day re-entry line (the floor + 25) does not move. The night-charge entities, the 21:30 notice and the card follow the plan.
- **The year.** Against 1.200.1 at the same scale, with 270 W: all 702 islanded-outage lines rise. At the year's own reserve readings, in a write mode, in advisory mode and with no eligibility passed, no line, setpoint, requirement, worst-case buy, cost ceiling, expected buy or expected write level falls. 156 written reserves rise at 0.944 (162 on nameplate): by 1 or 2 points at 4 h, up to 3 at 8 h, and on no 4 h night with a 16% floor in cost mode. 5 nights the caps hold under the higher line now disclose a shortfall (15 and 18 in advisory mode, the new line nights included); 3 holds charge; no charge holds. A cost night whose ceiling is at or above the new line's whole-percent ask writes and buys what it did (414 nights at 0.944, 417 on nameplate).
- **Ledger.** `cushion_standby_w`, `cushion_standby_basis` and `cushion_standby_kwh` record the figure, its basis and its energy (NULL where none applied, and on earlier rows): `cushion_kwh` − `cushion_standby_kwh` is the cushion without it. `CURRENT_ALGO_VERSION` stays 3: every verdict keeps its rule on the row's own line.
- **The measured-basis contract.** The planner still reads nothing of the runway's measured basis except through pinned entry points, now two. The second, `cushionStandbyOverheadW`, is pinned character for character and called once, before the first `await`; the planner reads its number once. The forbidden list now names the overhead rule, both overhead options and the marginal drain efficiency, as constants and as `limits` getters (the earlier pattern did not match `runwayPoolOverheadW`), and inventories pin who names them.

**What is disclosed**

- **The live plant.** Its line at today's settings (a 16% floor, 4 h) is 31–33%. The standby adds 1.08 kWh, about 1.2 points: 1.17 on nameplate (the basis until a pack ratio is measured), 1.24 at 0.944. In cost mode with its 90% ceiling, a night whose cost target is at or above the new line's whole-percent ask keeps its write (50% where the target is above that), its buy and its expected buy, and the cost force-charge to that target is unchanged. A resilience-bound night, or a cost night whose target sits between the old line and the new whole-percent ask, writes 1 point higher (32% to 33% on nameplate), 2 where the old line sat within 0.17–0.25 points under a whole percent: about 0.9 kWh more held per point, about 1 kWh more worst-case buy and up to 1 kWh more expected. At today's settings its line is far under 50%, so none of its nights meets the line force-charge.
- **More nights above 50%.** In a write mode the year's line nights (an ask above the 50% reserve) go from 37 to 53 at 0.944 (22 to 40 on nameplate). The 16 (18) that cross 50 are all 8 h nights. Each is armed: 7 (11) on the line force-charge, 9 (7) on a cost force-charge that already stops 10 points or more above the line. None is disclosed by the envelope and no expected close or expected buy falls; 2 nights on nameplate newly expect to close under their line, both already disclosed by the caps note. Where the line force-charge now runs, the night buys more and closes higher: up to 27.4 kWh more expected (2027-08-09 13:00 at 0.944, close 49% to 72.6%); over the year the crossing nights' 21:30 plans expect 58.5 kWh more (56.8 on nameplate). In advisory mode each is disclosed instead. Each such night carries 1.200.0's Limits: how a slot with Charge Now on behaves when the grid fails is unproven.
- **On a reserve already reading 50%.** On 1.200.1's stand the line nights go from 663 to 668 at 0.944 (659 to 666). The expected close falls on 110 (90) and the expected buy on 49 (44): the line walk is not monotone in its ask, and a line within 10 points of the cost ceiling hands a night from the cost force-charge to the line's own, whose level is the close it expects rather than the ceiling. 13 (9) newly expect to close under the line; each is disclosed. No line night in any state is under its line undisclosed.
- **Longer cushions.** With the year's 4 h nights set to 8–16 h, the line force-charge's walk, not monotone in its ask, gives some line nights a smaller expected buy or a lower expected close with the standby than without it: 11 plans at 0.944 at 8 h (8 on nameplate), 26–34 at 12–16 h (20–35), the expected buy by up to 9.2 kWh (2027-04-13 21:30 at 8 h: 40.8 to 38.77 kWh, close 74.4% to 70.3%). No armed line is disarmed, each night that then expects to close under its line is disclosed, and the written reserve and the worst-case figures never fall.
- **The 270 W is a pack-only fit.** On three later spans the implied overhead averaged 303 W (266–336). At 303 W the worked example's 15.02 kWh covers 3.97 h of the safety-factored load plus that drain.
- **The marginal drain efficiency.** The cushion's load keeps the planner's discharge efficiency. With `RUNWAY_DRAIN_MARGINAL_EFFICIENCY` set below 0.94 the runway drains faster than the cushion assumes; above it the cushion is the more conservative.
- **The walks leave the standby out.** The pre-window carry, the whole-house troughs and the expected buy still drain the panel load ÷ the discharge efficiency.
- **Above 1500 W.** Such a figure counts as 0 W, a smaller cushion for a larger figure. No option produces one: the floor option is clamped to 1500 W, the per-Core option to 500 W, and the panel has three slots.
- **Nothing here has run on the plant.** Its night plans are blocked by an incomplete calibration basis, so nothing is written and none of this has run on its charge path. Every figure here is the planner on the year of fixture nights.

### The morning summary of a cost night states how the night closed

- **What changed.** The morning summary of a night with no outage line whose force-charge rode it now states what ended the force-charge, the pool as read when the summary is sent, and the level the evening notice named. A night whose force-charge did not run, did not take effect, or was never confirmed short of its stop no longer goes under "completed" and the plain restore title.
  - A force-charge that ran: "reserve raised to 50%, and force-charge reached ~56.7% at 3:12am and was switched off there (~6.4 kWh expected); the pool read 57% at the close, against the ~56.7% the evening notice named". An OFF at the window end reads "ran toward ~56.7% until the window close"; any other gives its time and cause, after the expected figure: "ran toward ~56.7% (~6.4 kWh expected) and was switched off at 2:30am (the run backstop)". A re-issue of the ON adds ", after one re-issue of the ON".
  - A night like 2026-11-16: title "Night-charge: force-charge not applied on slot(s) 2, 3 — reserve restored to 16%", lead "closed with its force-charge not applied on every slot", and the body, after the failure's words, ", with no re-issue of the ON; the pool read 49% at the close, against the ~56.7% the evening notice named".
  - A failed or unconfirmed ON says what switched the force-charge off before the window did: "; it was switched off at 2:30am (the run backstop)", or, for a failed ON at its stop, "; the pool read ~56.7% at 12:20am and the force-charge was switched off there". Failed on every slot at its stop, the night goes under "force-charge ON not confirmed" in title and lead.
  - The other titles: "force-charge did not run" and "force-charge did not take effect" (lead "closed without its force-charge"), and "force-charge not confirmed". A force-charge that never started is not flagged when the pool read at the close is at or above its stop.
  - A slot the ON was not sent to is named: "; slot(s) 1 not switched on (named out of the backup pool by the pool alarm)".
  - A reading taken over 15 minutes after the close reads "the pool reads 61% now, past the window close"; with none, "the pool was not read at the close (no fresh panel reading)". An OFF time the clock cannot print is left out.
- **Why.** On 2026-11-16 a top-up that no slot applied closed at 49% against an announced 56.7% under the title "reserve restored" and the lead "completed". A short top-up that reaches its stop before the ON's readback check read "the panel never confirmed it"; it now reads "reached ~56.7% at 12:20am and was switched off there, with no readback showing every slot ON".
- **What does not change.** No write, no timing, no plan figure. A night whose record carries an outage line, a reserve-only night, a night a cancel or a grid loss ended, and the mid-day summary read as before. So does a night whose record gives no force-charge stop, and a force-charge that never started where the evening notice named a level other than that stop.
- **Known limits.**
  - The pool figure is the panel's whole-percent reading when the summary is sent, a minute or more after the close, beside a one-decimal level: no verdict word is used.
  - A re-issued slot is not named: the record keeps the count only.
  - A failure lists the slots still off at the check; one not listed may have been excused at the panel's ceiling, not charged.
  - A top-up needing under about 22 minutes of charge still switches on too late for a re-issue of its ON: the check comes 15 minutes after the ON, and none is made in the last 20 minutes of the window. The start rule is unchanged in this release.

## 1.208.0

### An alarm when a Core leaves the panel's backup pool, and a notice when it returns

**The alarm**

- **Why.** On 2026-11-14 at 23:35 the Smart Home Panel 2's backup pool capacity fell from 92.16 to 61.44 kWh: one Core's 30.72 kWh. For 44.5 hours that source slot delivered and took 0 W while the other two carried the house, the pool percent became the mean of two slots, and the panel went on listing three slots as connected with error code 0. The add-on logged, alerted, pushed and recorded nothing, at the loss or at the return on 2026-11-16 at 20:08. Its two slot rules (a non-zero `errorCodeNum`; connected with no hardware link) could not fire.
- **The rule.** Every REST reading of a panel is judged on the panel's own data: its pool capacity against the sum of its source slots' capacities. A shortfall of one whole slot or more, within 1,024 Wh, is the fault. Two more signals can name the slot and never raise alone: the pool percent matching the mean of the other slots, and a slot carrying under a fifth of its share of the load. The panel's flags are quoted and recorded and decide nothing, because their meanings are not established. No input is a Core's own telemetry, so a dark Core whose slot still carries the house is not a Core out of the pool.
- **When it raises.** After 6 readings spanning 10 minutes on which the percent or the power also reads a fault, or 15 readings spanning 15 minutes on the capacity alone. Of the 4,114 hourly rows in Home Assistant's statistics since 2026-05-26, 4,060 read 92.2 kWh, 44 read 61.4 kWh (this event) and the other 10 hold six earlier dips: four of minutes (one to 0.0 kWh, three to 61.4 kWh) and two of tens of minutes (to 30.7 kWh). Their causes are not established here, which is why the capacity alone waits the full 15 minutes; a drop that lasts a dwell raises whatever its cause, and ends when the capacity is whole again. A Core that leaves and rejoins three times inside an hour raises too. One healthy reading between fault readings does not restart the dwell; three spanning two minutes do.
- **What a raise states.** The count, the capacity the severity reads and the named slots each rest on a dwell of their own readings, never on the one reading that completes the dwell. The count is the smallest the dwell's readings support: a reading more slots short counts toward the dwell and starts a dwell of its own, so one reading at 30.7 kWh inside a one-Core dwell leaves the raise at "Core out", a warning, and "2 Cores out" follows only when readings two Cores short have met a dwell themselves. A slot is named in the title only from 6 readings naming it over 10 minutes; until then the title reads "slot not identified", the detail says what the readings so far point at ("2 readings since 23:10 point at slot 1"), and the name is added to the alert, with a log line and no second push, when its readings have reached that. Readings that only count the shortfall do not age a pending name out between the readings that name it, and a reading that names the slot in the title ends a pending rename, so two slots named by turns do not flip the title. A raise by the three-dips rule has no dwell behind it: its count is the smallest its third dip's readings support, and its capacity makes it no critical until readings at that count or above have met a dwell of their own (three whole readings over two minutes, or a gap of more than 15 minutes, start that dwell again).
- **What neither raises nor ends it.** A stale cloud shadow, a replayed body (the tracker keeps the fingerprints of the last 30 bodies itself, across restarts), an incoherent pool reading, a partial body, an offline panel, a restart, age. A reading that stays unusable is not ignored for ever: after 5 polls a capacity of zero counts as every slot out, a missing capacity as unknown and a missing connection flag as a slot the panel no longer lists. A reading that can be used but whose capacity cannot be compared with its slots (a slot with no capacity on record, a capacity above the reference, a shortfall that is not a whole slot) is said in the log when it first appears. After 30 minutes with no capacity verdict of either kind, while the panel is listed online and its data is not a stale shadow, one warning line names the reason.
- **The alert.** `shp2-pool-out`, titled for example "Core out of the backup pool: slot 1". The title and the slots in it are latched at the raise and change only on a change confirmed by a dwell of its own readings. The alert's id and the panel's name are latched at the raise too, and saved. The detail states the capacity read against the sum of the slots (and, when that reading is short by another count than the title's, which count the alarm stands at), when it first read short and the last full reading, the reading that named the slot, whether the slot is still listed and its percent, the panel's error code, error-list entries and differing flags as the panel sent them (an error-list entry as hex bytes with its index, with no meaning attached), the backup at the newest reading, and the grid. A second panel raises its own alert under its own id.
- **Severity.** A warning. Critical when the capacity a dwell has confirmed is under half the full pool, or when the grid is known absent on three consecutive readings: the add-on's grid verdict says absent, not merely unknown, and the panel's own grid flag agrees. An unknown grid holds the severity in force in both directions.
- **Delivery.** Pushed at any hour, at or above the notify minimum severity, with no quiet-hours hold and with the full detail as the push body: the morning digest prints titles only, so a loss at 23:46 would otherwise arrive at 07:00 as one line. The push is sent at the alert monitor's evaluation after the raise (one every 20 seconds); the monitor's 60-second push debounce does not apply to it, because the tracker has already held it through a dwell (a raise by the three-dips rule excepted: it has none). A send that fails is tried again after 20 seconds and then at waits that double to 10 minutes, until one succeeds; once delivered it leaves the digest's queue, on file too. A digest that goes out while the push is still failing names the alarm and does not stand in for the push: that is tried again at the next evaluation. An escalation to critical is pushed at the next evaluation, and after a send of it that fails it waits the same way. An alarm raised after the one on record has ended (a second loss while the first return's notice waits for the quiet hours, across a restart too) is pushed as a new alarm, on the tracker's own end record. The return notice waits for the notify quiet hours to end, as every resolve does. The choice is one function, `poolOutDelivery`, with one constant.
- **Speech.** The alert is voiced by the rules every alert follows: a critical, outside the broadcast's quiet hours. After the title the voice reads one short sentence of this alert's own ("Backup capacity reads 61.4 of 92.2 kWh.", and "No grid reported at 23:13." when the grid made it critical), not the first 200 characters of the detail: a critical says the title twice, and with the detail's lead the English message ran to 349–446 characters. The critical shapes the tests build (eleven of thirteen states, each said on the day, the next day and eight days on) are now 253 to 341 characters; where the limit of a spoken pass is set to 20 s the first of two passes has 17 s, which at the rate measured for 1.207.2 (318 characters in 12.8 to 13.9 s, about 1.5 s more after a change of voice) is 354. The push and the card keep the whole detail. The Spanish title says the count ("2 Cores fuera del respaldo"). The spoken message names one alert of its level, and this alert yields that place to every other: it is named when it is the only critical (or the only warning) the message can name. A Core can be out for days, and as a panel alert it would otherwise have outranked every grid, thermal and connectivity alert for all that time. "Backup at or below reserve" is not affected either way: it is spoken by its own announcer. While the alert stands, the broadcast's condition level is yellow for a warning and red for a critical, as for any standing alert of that severity.
- **The return.** The alarm ends only on evidence: the capacity whole for 18 readings over 30 minutes, or for 6 readings over 10 minutes once the returned slot is seen carrying its share or the pool percent vouches for it. Its resolve push is a notice of its own, "Core back in the backup pool: slot 1": since when the capacity is whole, when it first read short and how long before, the slot's watts and percent, and that the capacity the pool percent is a percent of changed between the two readings around the return (47 % and 64 % on 2026-11-16), so that step is not a measure of charging. An end reached just before the panel goes offline is announced at the panel's next fresh reading.
- **Across a restart.** The episode is saved when it is raised and restored at start: the alert stands at the first evaluation, before the panel has reported and before a device list has arrived (its id and the panel's name are on file), and is not pushed again. An episode restored with no push on record (a restart between the raise and its push) is pushed by the process that restored it, where every other warning standing at a start is taken as already announced. An alarm that ended before the restart while its resolve waited for the quiet hours is resolved afterwards with the return notice, from the end on file. That end on file is also what releases the boot sweep's hold on a panel with an empty slot, whose slots can never show "every slot of the panel". With the state file lost, the boot sweep's "Resolved" for this alert is held until the tracker has raised again or has seen 30 minutes of whole capacity with every slot of the panel listed as connected. A state file, or an entry of it, that could not be restored is said at warning level. A state file that cannot be read (damaged, or written by a later version) is kept as `pool-out.json.unread` beside the file the next save writes.
- **A Core removed on purpose** raises the same alarm, and the alarm stands: the text states what was read and names no cause, and no control accepts the smaller pool.

**Records**

- **Recorder.** New series per panel, on the existing cadence, retention and freshness gate (nothing during a stale shadow): `backup_full_wh`, `backup_full_raw_wh`, `backup_pct_raw`; per slot `src<n>_full_wh`, `src<n>_flags`, `src<n>_err`, `src<n>_ctrl`; `err_backup_n`, `err_master_n`, `err_hall_n`, `panel_flags`; and the tracker's `pool_counted_slots` and `pool_out_mask`. A flags value is `bits + 65536 × sent`, so a flag the panel did not send is never recorded as off; the reference lists the bit order.
- **Log.** One INFO line for each change of a flag, enum, code, count or capacity the panel reports (`pool-diag: slot 1 isPowerOutput 1 → 0`), the slot by number and never a serial. An error code and an error-list entry are logged in the reading they first appear in, before the source-error alarm's 3-minute debounce. The comparison is with the last values saved, so a restart neither invents a change nor loses one. The relay counters and relay states do not log a line each time they move (the master relays had switched 966, 967, 5,207 and 5,207 times by 2026-11-17): their change since the last line is said with the next line and at each tracker event. More than 12 changes in one reading are written as one line that lists them all. The tracker logs its reference, each streak, raise, set change and end with its four verdicts.
- **Captures.** At the first fault reading, the raise and the end, the panel's keys are written to `pool-events/` beside the database (20 files, at most 64 KiB each): an allowlist of the panel's backup and master blocks, the three error lists, the pool figures and slot watts, and the top-level scalars. A key ending `.sn`, and `chName`, `area`, `localTime` and `timeZone`, are never written, nor a string value shaped like an identifier, alone or inside a list. When more than 20 files exist the oldest first-fault captures are removed first, so the capture of a raise or an end is the last to go.
- **Debug view.** `GET /api/debug/pool-out`: per panel (by role, not serial) the reference, the four verdicts with their inputs, the streak, the episode and the newest reading as read; the capture files by index. It takes no parameter.

## 1.207.2

### The limit of a spoken alarm pass is an option; a pass leaves time for the other language

**Audible alarms**

- **What changed.**
  - **An option.** The time one spoken pass may take to render, 12 s since v1.47.5, can be set per install: `BROADCAST_TTS_PASS_TIMEOUT_MS`, 3000 to 60000 ms. Unset, it is 12000.
  - **A guard, for a limit above 12 s.** A pass may not use time that a pass still to render needs. With *m* passes still to render after it and *R* left of the 25 s spoken phase, an attempt (a first try, or the one retry after a failure of under 3 s) is handed min(limit, *R*, max(*R* − *m* × 8 s, 3 s)). One language, the last pass and every terminator have nothing behind them and keep min(limit, *R*); so does every attempt at 12 s or under.
- **An install that does not set the option is unchanged.** The default is 12 s, the option is not passed to the server while it is unset, and no reserve is held at 12 s or under. The test "INVARIANT 1" in `audioRendererBilingual.test.ts` holds the limits handed to the speech engine on thirteen paths (one language, two, and three passes; every attempt runs out; the first returns late; a dropped socket, then a retry, its pause on time, late or past the budget; with terminators) to the figures measured on v1.207.1.
- **Why.** Measured on the reference install on 2026-11-18 (silent renders, the voices in service): with its voice resident the English voice took 0.67 to 0.80 s per second of audio. A 318-character critical (18.4 s of audio) rendered in 12.8 and 13.9 s, a 289-character critical in 11.2 s, a 173-character notice in 6.8 s, and a charge-level sentence in 0.85 s, or 2.2 s after a change of voice. The Spanish voice rendered about 23 s of audio in 2.9 s, or 4.0 s after a change of voice. At 12 s the English pass of that 318-character critical is lost: on 2026-11-17 it was, and the tones and the Spanish pass played.
- **What a longer limit costs.** Nothing plays until the spoken phase ends, so render time is alarm delay.
  - **One language** (the shipped default). A pass that never returns holds the tones for the limit: 12 s by default, 20 s at 20000, 25 s from 25000 up.
  - **Two languages, the first pass late.** With an English pass ready at 14 s and a Spanish pass in 4 s: at 20000 both are spoken, the speech ready at 18 s; at 12 s the English pass is dropped, the speech ready at 16 s.
  - **Two languages, the first pass lost.** At 20000 it is cut at 17 s and the other has 8 s: one language after the 17 s and its own render time, or the tones alone at 25 s. By default the figures are 12 s, 12 s and 24 s. After a socket drop at 2.9 s the retry at 20000 has 13.8 s: what is left less 8 s.
  - **A terminator that never returns** holds the speech for its limit too: after a pass ready at 1 s the speech is ready at 13 s by default, at 21 s at 20000. While no English voice is named (the shipped default) its terminator is not kept across a restart.
  - The reserve is a fixed 8 s. The measured Spanish pass fits in it; a long English sentence rendered second can need more.
- **Not in this release.** Tones before speech. A per-sentence audio cache. A dropped pass is still recorded as a completed announcement and is not retried.
